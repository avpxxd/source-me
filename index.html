General Fact-Checking

Essential organizations and tools dedicated to verifying claims and debunking rumors.

  • Snopes

    One of the oldest and most trusted fact-checking sites on the internet, specializing in urban legends, rumors, and viral misinformation.

  • PolitiFact

    Focuses on looking at specific statements made by politicians and rating them for accuracy using their famous "Truth-O-Meter".

  • FactCheck.org

    A nonpartisan, nonprofit project of the Annenberg Public Policy Center that aims to reduce the level of deception and confusion in U.S. politics.

  • Reuters Fact Check

    Operated by Reuters, an international media agency, providing robust fact-checking of global news and social media claims.

Archival & Investigation

Tools to uncover deleted posts, track website changes, and access historical records.

  • The Wayback Machine (Archive.org)

    Explore billions of saved web pages over time. This is a crucial tool for finding deleted tweets, stealth-edited articles, or controversial websites that have been taken down.

  • Archive.today

    A time capsule for the internet. It takes a permanent 'snapshot' of a webpage that will remain online even if the original page disappears, is stealth-edited, or places content behind a paywall.

  • The Corroding Web

    Documenting and providing resources to protect you from online exploitation and bad actors

Education & Digital Literacy

Resources to build critical thinking skills for students, parents, and the general public.

  • MediaSmarts: Break the Fake

    Canadian educational materials, tip sheets, and videos designed to teach critical thinking and how to spot suspicious posts.

  • Zip Security: Avoiding Phishing & Clickbait

    A practical guide covering the basics and details of phishing, how to identify scams, and ways to recognize suspicious links and clickbait.

  • YouTube: Hit Pause

    A series of short, highly digestible videos designed by YouTube to teach media literacy, highlighting how to spot manipulation tactics and check sources before sharing.

  • UNICEF: Spotting Misinformation

    A practical, easy-to-read guide from UNICEF on how to quickly spot fake news, understand why people share it (like herd mentality), and how to talk to others about misinformation.
